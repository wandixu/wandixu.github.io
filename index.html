About Me

I'm a PhD candidate in computational chemistry at Johns Hopkins University, using molecular dynamics simulations, quantum chemistry calculations, and machine learning to understand how nanoscale structure drives biological and materials function.

My research sits at the intersection of physical chemistry and data science: I build and validate simulation models from scratch, from force-field parameterization and DFT to machine-learning regression on molecular data, and turn the results into design rules for experimental collaborators. I have contributed to 8 publications and preprints (two as first author) and peer-reviewed for ACS Materials Au, Nano Select, and Scientific Reports.

Outside the lab, I enjoy reading, paint in watercolor and acrylic, sketch, and stay active through hiking, tennis, badminton, swimming, and snowboarding.

Projects

Selected research projects and contributions.

01

Multiscale Lipid Vesicle Simulations

Built a DPD lipid model from MARTINI parameters and compared 12–60 nm vesicles at all-atom, MARTINI, and DPD resolution on structure (bilayer thickness, tail end-to-end distances, RDFs, Van Hove functions) and dynamics (Zilman–Granek fits against experimental relaxation data), using Kolmogorov–Smirnov statistics to quantify agreement. The DPD model reproduced all-atom results at roughly 2,700× lower cost.

GROMACS MARTINI DPD Python
Paper — RSC Advances 2026 →
02

Protein–Gold Nanoparticle Binding

Ran 36 all-atom trajectories (NAMD, 200 ns each) of six proteins — including cytochrome c, aprotinin, and ubiquitin — approaching a 4 nm MPA-coated gold nanoparticle from six orientations. Net charge decides whether a protein adsorbs and charge placement decides how: every adsorbing run converged on the same three-residue cationic patch.

All-atom MD CHARMM FF NAMD VMD
First-author paper in preparation →
03

Engineering Ligand Nanoparticles with Planar Lipid Surfaces

Constructing all-atom models of MUA- and MUTAB-functionalized gold nanoparticles using CHARMM parameters. Investigating structural and dynamical properties of ligand surfaces as a function of ligand composition and solution conditions.

All-atom MD Force-field parameterization Nanoparticles

04

Senolytic Drug Mechanism Studies

Contributed experimental work to four senotherapeutics studies — ferroptosis-inducing lipid senolytics (Cell Press Blue 2026), flavonoid senolytics (Aging Cell, accepted 2026), fucoidans enhancing SIRT6-dependent DNA repair, and DNA damage–driven NF-κB activation in aging (submitted to Nature Communications).

05

Polymer Dot–Protein Binding at Varying pH

All-atom MD of PFBT/PSMA semiconducting polymer nanoparticles (Pdots) binding ovalbumin across pH, resolving adsorption sites and conformational response for bioimaging and drug-delivery applications. Second author.

All-atom MD Polymer nanoparticles Protein adsorption
Preprint (submitted to Langmuir) →
06

DFT for Collaborators: MOF Anchoring & CuO Surfaces

Cluster DFT (ORCA) comparing anchoring modes of a Ce-doped Ti-MOF cluster on functionalized graphene — OH anchoring about 3× more stable than COOH — and periodic DFT+U (Quantum ESPRESSO) on bulk, surface, and oxygen-vacancy CuO for a nanotoxicity study, with Cu 3d PDOS explaining the higher reactivity of the defective surface. Delivered documented datasets and an open workflow.

ORCA Quantum ESPRESSO DFT+U PDOS
Workflow on GitHub →
07

Aqueous Solubility Prediction (ML, open source)

End-to-end regression pipeline (RDKit, scikit-learn, XGBoost) predicting aqueous solubility from molecular structure on the ESOL dataset. Scaffold-based splitting exposes 41–195% over-optimism in the naive random split; version-controlled, tested, and documented.
